Rebuilding the $161,040 a year claim line by line

The site promises a $161,040 yearly saving against hiring, which is the kind of figure most vendors round up from somewhere, so it is worth rebuilding from inputs published on the same page. A senior software engineer in the US runs about $18,290 a month fully loaded, a number built from benefits at 35 percent, payroll taxes at 15 percent, a 15 percent bonus, and 8 percent retirement stacked on base pay. Twelve of those months come to $219,480 before any code exists. Add the roughly $13,500 a company spends up front to source and screen that hire and the first-year bill is $232,980.

The subscription side of the same year prices at $5,995 a month, so twelve months is $71,940. The gap between the two first years is $232,980 minus $71,940, which is $161,040, the exact figure on the site. The advertised number assumes monthly billing all year. Take the yearly plan at $4,995 a month and the same comparison widens to $173,040; the quarterly plan at $5,495 lands at $167,040. The claim on the page is the smallest of the three, which makes it the conservative one.

The rebuild only holds when the alternative really is a full-time senior at a desk. If the actual need is a stream of scoped requests, one card active at a time through a Trello board, the subscription covers it for $71,940 and the seat was never going to be full. No pricing structure rescues the comparison when the workload does not fill one, so the number matters exactly as far as that judgment does.
